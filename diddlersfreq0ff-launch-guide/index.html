03Content rating & audience

Play Console runs the IARC questionnaire itself — you answer questions, it computes the rating per region. Answer honestly; here's what the honest answers are for this app.

  • When asked whether the app contains simulated gambling (slot machines, roulette, card games played for a fictional prize, using currency that isn't real money) — answer yes. This is exactly what the app does, disclosed on every game screen.
  • When asked whether real money or prizes of real-world value can be won — answer no.
  • Expect this to land the app at Teen or Mature depending on the rating board (ESRB/PEGI/etc. compute independently from the same answers) — that's normal and expected for this genre; it's the same bracket apps like Slotomania or DoubleDown Casino carry.
  • In the Target audience and content section, select an adult age range (18 and over), answer "no" to the app being directed at children, and do not enroll in Designed for Families — that program explicitly excludes simulated gambling.

06Closed testing kit

This is the step you're on now — everything below is aimed at getting the closed test live and tracking its clock.

a. Create the release. In Play Console: Testing → Closed testing → Create track (or your existing closed track) → Create new release. Upload:

android\app\build\outputs\bundle\release\app-release.aab

Give it a release name (e.g. 1.0 (1) — closed test) and short release notes, then save and review. Play Console will run its automated pre-launch checks before it lets you roll out — that can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few hours.

b. Add testers. On the track's Testers tab, create an email list (up to 100 addresses) or point it at a Google Group — either counts. You need 12 people who actually opt in, so it's worth inviting more than 12 in case a few don't follow through. Once saved, Play Console gives you an opt-in URL — that's what you send people.

Don't recruit exclusively from one household or one shared network. Google's automated review sometimes flags closed tests where all testers look like the same person (same device model, same IP range). A handful of different phones/networks is safer than 12 accounts opted in from your own living room.

08Things worth double-checking yourself

I'm not a lawyer, and gambling-adjacent app policy is exactly the kind of area where Google's own in-console guidance (which adapts to your specific answers and country list) should be the tie-breaker over anything written here:

  • Country availability. A handful of countries restrict simulated-gambling apps outright; Play Console will flag this during setup based on your target countries — read what it tells you there rather than guessing, and narrow your distribution list if it warns you about a specific country.
  • Category-specific requirements. Once you pick Games > Casino, Play Console sometimes surfaces extra declarations specific to that category — fill those out as they appear.
  • The keystore backup. Worth repeating: back up release.keystore and its password somewhere durable before you forget this page exists.
